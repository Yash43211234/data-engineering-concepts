# Dimension Modeling — 4-Step Design Process

## 1. Select the Business Process

First, understand **what the business does** and what we want to analyze.

Ask:

* What does the business do?
* What data/measurements do you want to analyze?
* What does the current operational data look like?
* What is the **end goal** of the analysis?

### Example — Retail

End goal:

> Analyze which products are selling, in which store, on which date, and under which promotion.

**Key idea:** Understand the business before designing the fact and dimension tables.

---

## 2. Declare the Grain

### What is Grain?

**Grain = Level of detail stored in one record of a fact table.**

Simply ask:

> **"What does one row represent?"**

Example:

If one row represents:

> One customer + one product + one date + one purchase

Then this is the **grain** of the fact table.

### Why is Grain Important?

The grain determines:

* What questions the fact table can answer
* Which columns should be present
* Which dimensions are required
* The size of the fact table

### Atomic vs Aggregated Data

**Aggregated grain:**

* Data is already summarized.
* Some detailed questions cannot be answered.

**Atomic grain:**

* Data is stored at the lowest useful level of detail.
* More detailed/ad-hoc questions can be answered.

> **Prefer the lowest useful/atomic grain when designing a fact table.**

⚠️ Lower grain → more records → more storage.

So there is a trade-off between **detail and storage**.

---

## 3. Identify the Dimensions

After declaring the grain, identify the dimensions needed to provide **context**.

For the retail example:

* `DIM_CUSTOMER` → Who?
* `DIM_PRODUCT` → What?
* `DIM_DATE` → When?
* `DIM_STORE` → Where?
* `DIM_PROMOTION` → Under which promotion?

Dimensions answer questions like:

> **Who, What, When, Where, Which?**

The required dimensions depend on the **business goal + grain**.

---

## 4. Identify the Facts

Facts are the **measurable values** that we want to analyze.

Examples:

* Quantity
* Sales Amount
* Discount
* Cost
* Profit

The fact table and its measures are decided based on the **declared grain** and business requirements.

### Multiple Fact Tables

Sometimes one fact table cannot represent the complete business process.

Examples:

* Sales
* Inventory
* Procurement

In such cases, we may need **multiple fact tables**.

---

# Derived / Calculated Facts

A **derived fact** is a measure that can be calculated from other stored values.

Example:

```text
Profit = Sales Amount - Cost
```

Suppose:

```text
Cost = ₹775
Sales Amount = ₹990

Profit = ₹990 - ₹775
       = ₹215
```

### Should we store derived facts?

Not always.

If a value can easily be calculated at query time, we can create a **VIEW** instead of physically storing the column.

```sql
CREATE VIEW sales_view AS
SELECT
    *,
    sales_amount - cost AS profit
FROM fact_sales;
```

### Why use a View?

* Physical table size remains smaller.
* Derived value is calculated when the query runs.
* Avoids storing redundant data.

---

# Overall Flow

```text
1. Select Business Process
          ↓
2. Declare Grain
          ↓
3. Identify Dimensions
          ↓
4. Identify Facts
```

### Important Dependency

```text
Business Goal
     ↓
   Grain
     ↓
  Facts
     ↓
Dimensions
```

The **grain is especially important** because it defines what one fact-table row represents.

---

# Interview Questions

### What is grain?

> Grain is the level of detail represented by one record in a fact table.

### Why is grain important?

> Because it determines what the fact table can answer and helps us decide the required facts and dimensions.

### What is an atomic grain?

> Atomic grain stores data at the lowest useful level of detail, allowing more detailed analysis.

### What is a derived fact?

> A derived fact is a measure calculated from other stored values, such as Profit = Sales − Cost.

---

# Remember

```text
Business Process → What are we analyzing?

Grain → What does one row represent?

Dimension → Provides context.

Fact → Provides measurements.

Derived Fact → Calculated from other facts.
```

**Most important:**

> **Always declare the grain before designing the fact table.**
